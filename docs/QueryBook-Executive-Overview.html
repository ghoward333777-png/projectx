Executive overview

The knowledge engine that cannot hallucinate.

QueryBook answers, translates, learns language and drafts prose from a provenance-tracked store of Fact Units. Every asserted fact carries a source; when the store has no answer, QueryBook refuses instead of inventing one. A large language model may help it phrase or brainstorm, but never decides what is true.

Deterministic · same input, same answer Provenance · cite or refuse No-LLM language understanding ~120 B per fact at scale
The guarantee

One covenant, enforced six ways

QueryBook is a single engine behind three products. Its value is the one axis a probabilistic model cannot hold: every answer is deterministic, sourced, reproducible, and free of invention. Six invariants make that a rule rather than a hope.

C1 · C2

Sourced or silent

No LLM grounds meaning or asserts a fact. Every stored fact carries a traceable source and a certification, or it does not exist.

C3 · C5

Deterministic identity

Meaning is a content fingerprint. The same fact, however phrased, resolves to one identity and is stored once. Same inputs always produce the same store.

C4 · C6

Refuse, don't guess

When nothing meets the trust threshold, the answer is an honest UNKNOWN. An LLM may only phrase verified facts or propose candidates the store then checks.

Language · translation · learning · generation

It learns language the way a child does — structure first, meaning second

All four language phases are deterministic and use no large language model. Each step writes provenance-tracked Fact Units you can audit, and a one-way Transition Gate keeps meaning suppressed until structure is stable.

Phase 1 · structural (SLPL)

Learns the shape of a language

Grapheme inventory, rule-seeded pronunciation, syllables, lexical stability and co-occurrence — with semantics switched off — until four readiness thresholds latch.

Phase 2 · semantic grounding

Attaches meaning from auditable sources

Meaning comes only from a bundled public-domain dictionary (Webster's 1913) and self-grounding against verified facts. No internet, no model.

Phase 3 · multilingual delta

Learns a second language as a delta

Reuses the English foundation and learns only the word mapping to Spanish or French from a bundled bilingual dictionary — each translation a sourced fact.

Phase 4 · speech

Pronounces, and speaks aloud honestly

Deterministic phoneme, syllable and stress analysis, plus real audio through the computer's built-in voice. If no voice engine is present it says so and never fakes audio.

ground("book") → english word "book" · means · "A collection of sheets of paper…" [Webster's 1913] acquire_language("es") → english word "house" · translation_es · casa [bilingual dict] pronounce("book") → /buːk/ · syllables 1 · stress 1 [rule-seeded G2P] "What is 1 + 1?" → VERIFIED 2 [1] arithmetic · sum · fp a1b2c3… "Who will win the 2031 election?" → UNKNOWN — no verified fact; QueryBook declines to guess.

Generation: fluent, but every sentence traces to a fact

QueryBook composes answers and prose from the facts it retrieved. A model may narrate them for tone and flow, under a hard rule: state only the listed facts, cite each one, add nothing. If a sentence has no backing fact, the composer flags it rather than shipping it. Fluency comes from the model; truth comes from the store.

Hypothesis & simulation — inside the boundary

It can wonder and simulate without ever defiling the database

QueryBook uses a model exactly where imagination helps and never where truth is decided. Hypotheses and simulations are proposals, quarantined and independently checked, so nothing a model dreams up can enter the UFCS/FQL store as fact.

ProposeA model proposes candidate facts (hypothesis) or projects a scenario's consequences (simulation). It acts only as a proposer.
QuarantineEach candidate is written to an isolated hypothesis or simulation domain at trust below the answer threshold.
VerifyEvery candidate is checked against the deterministic store — corroborated, contradicted, or open.
GateThe Prime-Directive gate answers only from verified facts. A low-trust proposal can never surface as an answer.
hypothesis: "Mars once had liquid water" domain=hypothesis trust=0.40 verify → corroborated by [3] facts ask: "Does Mars have life?" gate(min_trust 0.50) → UNKNOWN the 0.40 proposal is excluded — never asserted

The boundary holds by construction. There is no path from a proposal to an asserted fact except independent store verification. The store of record stays clean; the model gets to explore beside it, not inside it.

The parity ambition. Because proposals are archived with their verdicts, QueryBook can trend its creative output over time and measure it against a pinned reference model on a fixed benchmark — aiming for equal-or-better fluency and creativity while guaranteeing citations and zero fabrication. A new form of reasoning for LLMs

QueryBook as a reasoning organ a model can call

A language model reasons by association — fluent, broad, and unable to prove it is right. QueryBook offers a neomorphic reasoning mode: a new form of reasoning that is deterministic, provenance-bound, and verify-before-assert. A model reaches it as a tool during generation and gains three moves it does not have on its own.

Move 1 · deduce

Truth-preserving deduction

QueryBook computes a transitive closure over verified facts and asserts only what is entailed — each conclusion carrying its premises, trust decaying per hop so chains terminate. A proof, not a guess, and no model is involved.

Move 2 · hypothesize

Grounded exploration

The model proposes; QueryBook adjudicates each proposal against the store as corroborated, contradicted, or open. The model gets to speculate with a scorekeeper that never lets a speculation pass as fact.

Move 3 · verify

Cite-or-refuse checking

The model hands QueryBook any claim; it returns the claim with a citation, a contradiction, or an honest unknown. The model keeps its fluency and inherits an auditable floor.

llm → qb.deduce() premises: sparrow is_a bird [1] bird is_a animal [2] qb → sparrow is_a animal trust 0.96 [from 1,2] llm → qb.verify("drug X interacts with Y") qb → UNKNOWN — not in the licensed store model must withhold this claim
AccessExposed as a callable interface — function-calling or an MCP-style tool — so any model can reach QueryBook mid-generation and weave the sourced result into its answer.
DivisionThe model brings breadth, framing and fluency; QueryBook brings deduction, memory with provenance, and the refusal to assert what it cannot support.
BoundaryThe model calls in but cannot write facts: results are read, deductions are proven, proposals stay quarantined. The store of record is never touched by the model.

Why it is a new form. It gives a probabilistic model a deterministic, reproducible, auditable reasoning faculty with a memory that cannot hallucinate — a complement to the model's intuition, not a replacement for it.

Three products, one engine

Q&A app · Chatbot · Middleware

The same guarantee ships in three shapes: a destination people ask, a conversation they hold, and a layer that sits under the models they already run. Scenarios below are illustrative — QueryBook is a proven prototype, not yet a deployed product.

Product 1

Q&A application

Ask a question, get a cited answer or an honest "unknown."

A grounded search-and-answer surface for a body of knowledge an organization owns or trusts — policies, standards, records, catalogs. Every answer names its sources; anything unsupported is refused, not smoothed over.

Example"What is the notice period in the 2024 lease?" → the clause, cited to the document, or UNKNOWN if the store has no such lease.
CaseIllustrative — public-records desk. A city publishes a QueryBook Q&A over ordinances and meeting minutes. Residents get answers that link to the exact record; when a record does not exist, the app says so rather than paraphrasing a rumor. Staff stop re-answering the same lookups.
ImpactDeflects repetitive lookups, removes the liability of a confident wrong answer, and turns an archive nobody reads into a service people trust.
Product 2

Chatbot

A conversation that stays honest across turns.

The same engine in dialogue. A model carries tone and continuity; QueryBook carries truth. The bot answers from verified facts, shows competing values when the record disagrees, and declines when it does not know — turn after turn.

Example"What's your refund window, and does it cover opened items?" → the policy, cited; if the opened-items rule is not in the store, it says that part is not established rather than inventing one.
CaseIllustrative — support assistant. A retailer's chatbot answers only from published policy Fact Units. When a shopper pushes for an exception the policy does not cover, it refuses and offers a handoff, instead of promising a refund the company never authorized.
ImpactCuts the invented-promise risk that keeps legal teams from deploying support bots, while still deflecting routine volume.
Product 3

Middleware

A verification layer under the models you already run.

QueryBook sits between an application and its language model. The model drafts fluently; QueryBook checks each factual claim against a trusted store, strips or flags whatever it cannot verify, attaches citations, and enforces refusal on the rest. Reasoning, provenance and a hallucination guardrail become a layer, not a rewrite.

Flowprompt → LLM draft → QueryBook verify each claim → cited answer · flagged gaps · or refuse
CaseIllustrative — clinical drafting aid. A model drafts a patient-friendly summary; QueryBook checks every dose and interaction against a licensed formulary store. A plausible-sounding but unverified interaction is flagged and withheld; the verified content ships with citations a pharmacist can audit.
ImpactLets regulated industries adopt generative fluency without accepting generative risk — the model's reach with an auditable floor under it.
Industry impact

Where "cite or refuse" changes what can ship

The guarantee matters most where a confident wrong answer is expensive — legally, clinically, or financially. In those sectors the blocker to deploying AI is not fluency; it is trust. QueryBook targets exactly that gap.

Healthcare

Drug, dosage and guideline answers verified against licensed sources; unverifiable claims withheld with an audit trail.

Legal & compliance

Every statement cited to a clause or filing; "no basis found" instead of a fabricated citation.

Financial services

Policy, disclosure and product answers that can be reproduced and audited to the exact source.

Government & public sector

Records and benefits questions answered from the record itself, with refusal where the record is silent.

Education

Language learning and tutoring on a sourced base, plus deterministic pronunciation and translation.

Enterprise knowledge

Internal search that answers from owned documents and declines outside them — no leakage into invention.

Economic impact

Cheap to run, expensive to be wrong without

Two economic stories. First, the cost of being wrong — the rework, liability and lost trust a hallucinated answer creates — is what QueryBook removes. Second, the cost of running it is low, because a Fact Unit is small. Figures below are illustrative projections with stated assumptions, not measured results.

Where the value comes from

Value driver

Hallucination-cost avoidance

Each confident wrong answer can cost rework, a bad decision, or liability. Refusing the few percent of answers a system cannot support removes that tail entirely.

Value driver

Audit & compliance savings

Provenance is built in, so "show me the source" is a fingerprint lookup, not a manual review. That is the difference between shippable and not in regulated settings.

Value driver

Deflection at low cost

Routine questions answered from the store — with a small storage and compute footprint — convert an unread archive into round-the-clock self-service.

Value driver

Model spend, contained

As middleware, the model runs once to draft; QueryBook does the verifying deterministically. No repeated model calls to second-guess an answer.

Illustrative unit economics. Assume a support desk handling 1,000,000 questions a month, 60% of which are routine lookups, and assume each hallucinated answer that reaches a customer carries an expected cost of a few dollars in rework and risk. A guarantee that those 600,000 routine answers are cited-or-refused, at a storage cost measured in tens of gigabytes, changes the deploy/no-deploy decision that fluency alone could not. Numbers here are assumptions for illustration, not a forecast.
