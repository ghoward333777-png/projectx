AI Storage Efficiency

AI is hungry for more than chips. Models, training data, checkpoints and memory stores all have to live somewhere, and smart design can shrink every one of them.

AtomicGraph · Issue 01 · 2 min read

Talk about AI costs usually stops at processors. Storage is the quieter bill. It grows with every model version saved, every document indexed and every conversation remembered.

What takes up the space

  • Model weights. A model with billions of parameters occupies many gigabytes, and each fine-tuned variant is traditionally another full copy.
  • Training data and checkpoints. Datasets are kept for reproducibility, and training runs save their progress again and again.
  • Vector stores. To let AI search documents by meaning, each passage is turned into a long list of numbers called an embedding. Millions of passages mean billions of numbers.
  • Logs and history. Prompts, answers and audit trails pile up quickly.

Shrinking the model

Quantization, which stores each weight in fewer bits, cuts model files by four to eight times. Adapters go further for customised models. Instead of saving a whole new copy for each customer or task, you save a small add-on file that modifies one shared base model. A hundred variants then cost little more than one.

Shrinking the memory

Embeddings can be compressed as well, by using shorter vectors or by storing coarse codes in place of full-precision numbers. Search quality drops slightly and storage drops a lot.

A different approach is to store meaning once. If a thousand documents all state that a pump was replaced on a given date, a text index stores that sentence a thousand times. A structured store, such as a knowledge graph, records the fact once and links it to its thousand sources.

Store the fact once and point to it a thousand times.

Housekeeping

The unglamorous measures pay well: remove duplicate data, delete checkpoints nobody will reload, and move rarely used material to cheaper, slower storage. One caution applies. Anything needed to reproduce or audit a result should be kept, so decide what that is before the clean-up, not after.

Storage efficiency is also speed. Smaller models load faster, smaller indexes search faster, and data that fits in memory avoids the slowest step of all: waiting for the disk.
