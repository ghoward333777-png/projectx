Key findings

  • Compression: 4 of 6 encodes landed within 10% of their 2500 kbps target (container bitrate); over target: motion H264 at 3245 kbps, motion H265 at 3247 kbps (the VBV buffer starts full, which lets a short, complex clip run past the cap, plus about 4% MPEG-TS overhead). Raw video shrank 102.2–131.4×.
  • Quality at the same target, studio source: H264 34.84 dB PSNR / 0.9701 SSIM; H265 34.87 dB PSNR / 0.9696 SSIM; AV1 41.68 dB PSNR / 0.9752 SSIM. SVT-AV1 runs PSNR-tuned while x264/x265 tune for perceived quality, so compare SSIM across codecs and PSNR within one.
  • Quality at the same target, motion source: AV1 27.86 dB PSNR / 0.9167 SSIM; H264 25.84 dB PSNR / 0.8551 SSIM; H265 27.66 dB PSNR / 0.9127 SSIM. SVT-AV1 runs PSNR-tuned while x264/x265 tune for perceived quality, so compare SSIM across codecs and PSNR within one.
  • Transport efficiency: UFCS-FQL framing kept 99.612–99.912% of bytes as payload; with modelled TCP/IP + Ethernet overhead 93.78–94.06%. ACK traffic back was 0.047–0.289% of the forward bytes.
  • Path LAN · 1 Gbps · 0.5 ms: 1280x720 at 2500 kbps, goodput 558.94 Mbps (56.1% of the link), chunk delivery p50 32.23 ms, path transit p50 12.49 ms.
  • Path Regional · 50 Mbps · 20 ms: 1280x720 at 2500 kbps, goodput 45.68 Mbps (91.7% of the link), chunk delivery p50 301.66 ms, path transit p50 25.72 ms.
  • Path Long-haul · 20 Mbps · 80 ms: 1280x720 at 2500 kbps, goodput 17.88 Mbps (89.7% of the link), chunk delivery p50 772.21 ms, path transit p50 80.57 ms.
  • Path Mobile · 3 Mbps · 40 ms: 960x540 at 1500 kbps, goodput 2.84 Mbps (94.9% of the link), chunk delivery p50 2961.41 ms, path transit p50 41.75 ms.
  • Decompression: on one CPU core, studio H264 decodes 14.9× real time; studio H265 decodes 8.8× real time; studio AV1 decodes 15.4× real time; motion H264 decodes 10.3× real time; motion H265 decodes 6.5× real time; motion AV1 decodes 10.8× real time.
  • RTMP live over Regional · 50 Mbps · 20 ms (per-priority connections): edge → Node 2 p50 49.39 ms / p95 84.72 ms; lag above its best 289.6 ms at the end (worst 519.6 ms). The edge, before any transport, already showed 443 ms at the end (worst 674.4 ms), so this lag comes from the live encoder falling behind real time; the transport added 0 ms; arrived intact.
  • Direct live over Long-haul · 20 Mbps · 80 ms (per-priority connections): edge → Node 2 p50 138.72 ms / p95 184.23 ms; lag above its best 34.9 ms at the end (worst 107 ms); arrived intact.
  • Direct live over Long-haul · 20 Mbps · 80 ms (one shared connection): edge → Node 2 p50 596.44 ms / p95 642.66 ms; lag above its best 57.4 ms at the end (worst 127 ms); arrived intact.

4 · Propagation

Where each frame's time goes, joined from the two nodes' traces (same host clock). Queue: enqueued until its first byte left Node 1. Serialisation: first to last byte at the shaped rate. Path: last byte out until Node 2 had the whole frame (propagation delay plus the emulator's serialisation). ACK return: Node 2 receipt until Node 1 saw the acknowledgement. VOD files are queued all at once, so their queue time grows through the file; live chunks are queued as the encoder produces them.

Method and limits

  • Both nodes ran as separate processes on one host (4 cores), so their clocks are shared and one-way times are exact. Paths are emulated by a relay that adds one-way delay and a capacity cap in both directions; it does not drop or reorder data, so loss and retransmission are not measured.
  • Sources are synthetic: generated graphics with sensor noise, and a Mandelbrot zoom for high motion. The added noise is not compressible, which keeps PSNR modest at these bitrates; real camera footage usually scores higher at the same rate.
  • VOD encodes use ffmpeg with a target bitrate from the link plan (libx264 / libx265 veryfast, SVT-AV1 preset 10). Live encodes use x264 zerolatency at 2.5 Mbps with a 1 s GOP; the RTMP edge flushes every 64 KiB or 200 ms, the direct feed every 100 ms.
  • Quality is PSNR (luma and average) and SSIM against the lossless master, frames aligned by index. Live feeds loop the master and are compared over its first pass. Media decode cost is measured single-threaded.
  • The TCP/IP overhead column is a model (90 bytes per 1,448-byte segment); everything else is measured.
