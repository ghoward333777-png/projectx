      Listen & analyse
      
        
      Record or upload speech. The app measures how it was said — volume, pauses, speed, pitch and tone, tremble, hoarseness, breath — recognises the vocal mode (whispering, crying, laughing, shouting, singing or speaking) and links the words to what the builder has learned.
      
        
      
          
          
          
          
        
      
        
      
        
      
        
      
          
          
        
      
        
      
          
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
        
      🎧 Live captions and audio files
      
        
      Microphone: speak and the words appear as you talk — a quick grey draft from the browser where it can, replaced by Google Speech-to-Text a moment later (the speech is cut at your pauses, at most every 6 seconds). When you stop, the recording and its transcript can be downloaded or ingested. Audio files: MP3, M4A, OGG, WAV — any length.       Ask by voice
      
        
      Ask a question out loud (or type it) — for example “how do you say house in Spanish”, “what does tree mean”, “translate good morning to French”. The app understands the words and how you said them, answers from what the builder learned, and speaks the answer with the voice profile above. With “respond to the mood” on, a whispered question is answered in a whisper and a tearful one gently.
      
        
      
        
      
          
          
          
      
        
      
        
      🤫 Silent question — lips only for people who cannot speak aloud
          
      Face the camera and mouth the question without sound. Lips show only part of speech, so the app matches them to the questions it answers and shows the best readings — tap the right one. Guided is the most reliable: tap the kind of question and the language, then mouth just the word.

        Visual Speech Engine — full-face lip sync — speech science, no AI model, same answer every time
        
        
        From the words and their recording: the sounds and their timing, pitch and loudness, an emotion estimate and a voice fingerprint (audio analysis); then the mouth moving the way people really speak — each sound blends into its neighbours, lips round ahead of an “oo”, every p, b and m closes — with the jaw, brows that lift on stressed words, small head nods, blinks and glances at pauses (face motion). Your own mouth shapes are used once the reservoir (step 5) has learned them. Exports drive real rigs: ARKit-named blendshape curves for Blender, Unreal, Unity or Live Link.
        
        
        
          
          
          
          
          
        
        
        
        
          
        
            
        
          
        
        
        
        
        How accurate is it?
        
        
        Measures lip-reading by this engine against lip-reading v1 — on synthetic speakers (each with their own mouth shapes, speed and tracking noise) and on your own held-out recordings from the reservoir.
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        Object scan — recognize, follow and zoom — for ingestion and questions
        
        
        Recognizes 80 kinds of everyday object in the picture above — people, cars, cups, phones, books, dogs… — on this computer (MediaPipe Object Detector, EfficientDet-Lite0, bundled; nothing is uploaded). Each object is followed from frame to frame. Click a box (or name a kind) to follow it and see it enlarged. What was seen is saved as numbers — what, when, how sure, where in the picture — never the picture itself, and you can ask about it later. Recognized objects are observations, not facts.

        2 · Analyse: sync check and lip-reading
        
        
        Plays the recording or file once through the face tracker, then checks the picture against the recommended set-up, measures whether sound and lips are in sync, and reads the lips. A script makes the check far more reliable: lips show only about a third of speech.
        
        
        
        
          
          
          
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        3 · Overdub: new words in the speaker's timing
        
        
        Speaks new words with the voice chosen on the Speak tab and fits them into the speaking time of the video, phrase by phrase, without changing the pitch. Every result is labelled SYNTHETIC VOICE.
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        4 · Ingest into QueryBook (sound + face)
        
        
        Takes in the analysed video the way QueryBook takes in speech — words, meaning, how it was said — and adds what the camera sees: the lips choose between possible readings of the words, the face settles whether it is crying or laughing, and a speaker check says whether the voice belongs to this face. The script above, if given, is one of the readings.
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        5 · Teach and improve — the ingestion reservoir (speech-visual domain)
        
        
        Every recording with a face and known words goes into the reservoir of the ingestion domain (numbers only — never the picture). The app learns from it: how this camera sees these mouths, which shapes they confuse, this computer's camera/microphone delay, the lip-sync timing, and the talking face's mouth shapes. About one recording in four is held back for testing; a learned version is switched on only when it beats the current one on those held-back recordings. Every version is kept and can be rolled back.
        
        
        
        
        Teach it (about 2 minutes)
        
        
        Face the camera as in the guide above, press the button, read the sentence aloud, press again. Twelve sentences cover every mouth shape. With Google Speech-to-Text connected, each sentence also gets exact word times, which tune the lip-sync timing.

        Photo Real Lab — an AI narrator that looks and sounds like the author
        
      
        The method: record one perfect sample — the author reading the reference passage on camera. The lab fits a model of the author's own mouth to it (how far the lips lead the sound, how fast they move, the exact shape for every sound) — the measured reason it looks right. Every narration, from any face method, is then measured frame by frame against what the author's mouth would do for the same words, and released only if it passes. The photo-driven face is calibrated in a closed loop until it passes. Every result is a QueryBook fact in the photoreal graph domain.

        Verifying now
        
    
        
      
        What the verification proves
        
      
          
        
        1. It starts from nothing — a brand-new empty database: 0 facts, 0 words read.
          2. 
        
        2. Nothing comes from outside — every network connection is blocked for the whole run; the code may import only the Python standard library (checked line by line).
          3. 
        
        3. Sub-language before language — asking for meanings before Phase 1 must be refused.
          4. 
        
        4. It learns step by step — the learning curve is recorded after every reading, from the first word.
          5. 
        
        5. Same inputs, same result — everything learned is reduced to one SHA-256 fingerprint. It must equal this computer's own build, and anyone who runs the same program gets the same fingerprint.
          6. 
      
        
      
        Limits, stated plainly: English structure (Phase 1) is learned from raw text alone. Meanings and the other 16 languages are taken from the declared reference files — Webster's 1913 dictionary and the MUSE bilingual word lists (published by Meta AI researchers) — so the proof shows that everything is computed only from those files, not who compiled them. Their hashes are listed below so anyone can obtain and compare the same files.

        4. Their fingerprint must be identical to yours. Any change to the code, the starter text or the word lists changes it.

        What this does
        
      
        This is the administrator's tool that builds QueryBook's languages before anyone runs queries. It is automatic: the first time it starts, it builds every language by itself. Build all languages runs the whole build again; nothing has to be chosen.
        
      
        How the languages are learned
        
      
          
        
        1. English sub-language (Phase 1). It reads English for structure only — letters, sounds by phonics rules, syllables, rhythm, which words recur and which go together — with meanings switched off.
          2. 
        
        2. Transition Gate. A one-way gate opens only when sounds found ≥ 0.80, common words settled ≥ 0.70, word pairs ≥ 1.5 per word and new patterns < 0.20.
          3. 
        
        3. Meanings (Phase 2) from Webster's Unabridged Dictionary (1913, public domain).
          4. 
        
        4. Links (Phase 3) from each learned English word into the 16 other languages.
          5. 
        
        5. Pronunciation (Phase 4) by phonics rules (and espeak-ng when installed).
          6. 
        
        6. Each other language — Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Czech, Greek, Romanian, Hungarian, Catalan — its words, sounds and links.
          7. 
      
        
      
        No AI model is used. Every learned fact keeps its source. The same inputs always give the same result.
        
      
        Speech
        
      
          
        
        • Speak — one voice profile (emotion and strength, tone, accent, speed, pitch, volume, pauses, articulation, tremble, hoarseness, breathiness, whisper, voice age) is sent to any engine: espeak-ng, the prototype's formant voice, Windows or macOS voices (all offline), Google Gemini TTS, Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Microsoft Azure or Amazon Polly. Advanced shows every parameter of the chosen engine. The table “How each control is handled” says, for each control, whether the engine does it natively, QueryBook adds it afterwards, or it is not available; the exact request is shown with keys removed.
          • 
        
        • Match a target — give the output you need (pitch, speed, loudness, pauses, tremble, breath). The app generates, measures the result, adjusts and repeats until it matches.
          • 
        
        • Live captions from the microphone — speak and the words appear as you talk (a grey draft from the browser, replaced by Google Speech-to-Text about a second or two later); stop to download or ingest the recording and transcript.
          • 
        
        • Transcribe an audio file — MP3, M4A, OGG or WAV of any length: live captions while it plays (Google Speech-to-Text hears it in pieces cut at pauses, ahead of the voice), or the whole file in the background — in pieces with the Google key, or by Google's long-audio recognition through Cloud Storage. Click a time to jump there; download TXT, SRT, VTT or JSON; ingest it.
          • 
        
        • Listen & analyse — measures a recording and recognises the vocal mode: whispering, crying, laughing, shouting, singing or speaking, with the reasons. Hoarseness, tremble and voice age are judged on a 3-second “aaah”, as voice clinics do.
          • 
        
        • Ask by voice — understands a spoken question and how it was asked, answers from what the builder learned, and can answer a whisper in a whisper.
          • 
        
        • Talking face — after Speak or Ask, a face (2D, or 3D with Enter VR on a headset) moves its mouth in time with the voice. The mouth shapes are the 15 standard visemes, timed from the measured audio (or exactly, from Google Speech-to-Text word times). Export as JSON, CSV or Rhubarb TSV for animation tools.
          • 
        
        • Teach and improve — Face & video, step 5. Recordings with a face and known words go into the ingestion reservoir (numbers only, never pictures). The app learns this camera's and this speaker's mouth shapes, the shapes they confuse, the computer's camera/microphone delay, the lip-sync timing and the talking face's mouth shapes. A new version is used only when it does better on held-back recordings; all versions are kept and can be rolled back; the reservoir can be exported and imported to pool learning between computers.
          • 
        
        • Camera with the voice — in Ask by voice and Listen & analyse, tick the camera box: the lips choose between the recognizer's possible readings of your words, the face settles close calls (crying and laughing sound alike but look opposite), the answer's voice follows the face's mood, and a speaker check says whether the voice belongs to the face.
          • 
        
        • Face & video — camera, screen or video file. Live face tracking with a quality check, a sync check (how many milliseconds sound and lips are apart), lip-reading with look-alike words, checking the lips against a script, an avatar that copies the mouth, and overdubbing (with consent; always labelled synthetic).
          • 
        
        • Speech log — every speech process with UTC timestamps and stage timings to the hundredth of a second; time statistics per process and engine; CSV download.
          • 
        
        • Honest limits: cloud engines need your own keys; their requests follow each provider's published format and are tested against a stand-in server, not against the live services. The vocal-mode rules are tested on real normal speech and on synthetic whispering, laughing, sobbing and singing; real crying and laughing recordings vary, so close calls are marked “uncertain”.
          • 
      
        
      
        Photo Real Lab and the author's AI narrator
        
      
          
        
        • Author persona — a photo, a voice sample and the author's signed consent (fingerprints of both samples are kept). Voice: Google custom (cloned) voice, ElevenLabs clone, or the offline voice tuned to the sample. Face: the photo-driven face here, HeyGen, D-ID, or a local model (Wav2Lip, SadTalker or any command).           •

          • 
        
        • The photoreal graph — every measurement and setting is a QueryBook fact (UFCS 1.0 packet) in the “photoreal” domain; export it to the QueryBook store.
          • 
        
        • Answer as the author — in Ask by voice choose the narrator under “Answer as”.

        Silent questions (lips only)
        
      
        In Ask by voice, open Silent question: mouth the question without sound and tap the right reading. Guided is the most reliable: tap the kind of question and the language, then mouth only the word; words that look the same on the lips are shown together. Teaching the app your mouth (Face & video → Teach and improve) makes it read you better.
        
      
        Filming for lip-sync and lip-reading
        
      
          
        
        • What to capture: the whole face, forehead to chin, filling 40–60% of the picture's height — not just the mouth. The jaw, cheeks and chin carry speech too, and the tracker needs the whole face to find the lips.
          • 
        
        • Mouth size: at least 150 pixels wide in the picture.
          • 
        
        • HD or 4K? 1080p is best; 720p works. 4K adds file size, not accuracy (the tracker works on a small copy of each frame).
          • 
        
        • Frame rate: 60 per second is best — lips move in 50–100 ms; 30 is the minimum.
          • 
        
        • Angle: face the camera, within about 20° left/right and 15° up/down.
          • 
        
        • Light: even, from the front (window or lamp behind the camera); nothing bright behind the head.
          • 
        
        • Nothing over the mouth: no hand, microphone or mask.
          • 
        
        • Screen capture: play the video full size at normal speed and share the tab with its audio.
          • 
        
        • Sound: microphone 20–30 cm away in a quiet room.
