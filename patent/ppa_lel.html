Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to machine learning systems for natural language acquisition and speech production. More particularly, it relates to a hierarchical, developmentally-sequenced language learning architecture — the Language Expression Layer (LEL) — that acquires linguistic capabilities from a structured training corpus without reliance on external dictionaries, rule-based modules, or language packs; a sub-language priming layer establishing perceptual and structural prerequisites before language learning begins; a transition gate governing progression from structural to semantic learning; an accelerated multilingual acquisition model exploiting first-language structural transfer; a multi-stage speech output pipeline producing natural speech from internalized learned parameters; and the co-grounded integration of the LEL with a Federated Query Language (FQL) and Universal Federated Connectivity System (UFCS).

Background of the Invention

[0001] Conventional natural language processing systems acquire linguistic capability through (1) rule-based systems encoding grammar, morphology, syntax, and pronunciation via manually authored rules, dictionaries, and language packs; or (2) statistical and neural language models trained top-down on tokenized text, deriving associations between tokens without grounding in sub-linguistic or perceptual foundations.

[0002] Both paradigms have fundamental limitations. Rule-based systems are brittle, language-specific, and do not generalize beyond their rules. Neural models trained top-down on text lack grounding in the acoustic, prosodic, and communicative substrates from which human language emerges. Neither replicates the developmental sequence of human language acquisition.

[0003] Human language acquisition proceeds developmentally: an infant performs sub-linguistic perceptual processing — discriminating acoustic signals, detecting prosody, segmenting continuous sound — before apprehending semantics. Words are first acquired as stable acoustic objects. The understanding that words serve communication emerges as a discovered insight, not a pre-loaded assumption. Grammar and syntax develop subsequently as tools serving communication.

[0004] Conventional systems also treat multilingual capability as parallel resource construction — separate dictionaries, grammars, and pronunciation databases per language — failing to exploit the structural transfer by which knowledge of a first language accelerates acquisition of later languages.

[0005] Existing speech synthesis relies on externally authored pronunciation lexicons, rule-based phoneme-to-grapheme mappings, and separately trained prosody and vocoder modules — engineered rather than learned.

[0006] There exists a need for a language acquisition and speech production system that: (a) replicates the human developmental sequence from sub-linguistic foundations to emergent communicative and semantic competence; (b) internalizes all linguistic features as learned parameters within a unified model, without external dictionaries, rule engines, or language packs; (c) exploits first-language structural transfer to accelerate multilingual acquisition; (d) produces natural speech through an integrated learned pipeline; and (e) co-grounds linguistic understanding with structured federated data query operations.

Summary of the Invention

The invention provides a system and method for hierarchical developmental language acquisition and integrated speech output in a federated query system, comprising: (1) a Sub-Language Priming Layer establishing acoustic, phonemic, prosodic, communicative, and information-theoretic foundations before language learning; (2) a Language Expression Layer (LEL), a trained model acquiring all linguistic capabilities as learned parameters from a structured multilingual corpus; (3) a Transition Gate of four measurable readiness thresholds governing progression from structural-acoustic to semantic-communicative learning; (4) a Multilingual Transfer (Delta Acquisition) model reusing first-language foundations to accelerate later languages; (5) a Speech Output Pipeline of sequentially integrated learned stages; and (6) a Co-Grounded Integration architecture linking the LEL to FQL and UFCS for language-agnostic query execution.

No large language model acquires language, grounds meaning, or asserts a fact. Unlike conventional systems, the invention acquires and performs language understanding without any large language model (LLM). The LEL learns its own parameters bottom-up from the Sub-Language Priming Layer rather than being trained on, distilled from, or querying a pre-existing LLM; and in the reduced-to-practice embodiment (Section VII) semantic grounding is performed deterministically, drawing word meanings only from a bundled public-domain dictionary and by self-grounding against verified Fact Units in the store, each meaning carrying an auditable source. An LLM is expressly excluded from the language-understanding and grounding path (see FIG. 8), and no fact asserted by the system originates from an LLM. An LLM may, however, be used elsewhere in strictly non-asserting roles: (i) to phrase or narrate an answer over facts the system has already verified, and (ii) to propose hypotheses and drive simulations for exploration — in each case the LLM never establishes truth, and any candidate it proposes is admitted only after independent verification against the deterministic store (an optional grounding “suggestor” is admitted only where it agrees with the public-domain dictionary, which remains the authority).

Detailed Description of the Preferred Embodiments

Section I — The Sub-Language Priming Layer (SLPL) (FIG. 2)

[0007] The SLPL is a pre-training stage executing prior to language corpus ingestion, establishing the perceptual prerequisites that make language learnable. It comprises six components, each producing a defined, measurable representation audit: (a) Acoustic Phonetic Processing — learns to discriminate sounds at the signal level (formant, pitch, duration, amplitude), producing a discrimination-thresholded feature embedding; (b) Phonics and Phonemic Awareness — learns sound-symbol correspondence, segmentation, blending, and minimal-pair discrimination, producing a discrete phoneme-boundary segmenter with a measured minimal-pair rate; (c) Communication Science Foundations — learns turn-taking and signal-versus-noise discrimination, producing a per-segment communicative-salience weight without assigning meaning; (d) Prosody and Rhythm Processing — learns stress, intonation, timing, and pause as pre-semantic carriers, producing a prosodic contour vector; (e) Information-Theoretic Grounding — acquires an entropy/redundancy weighting allocating representational attention by measured information density; and (f) Neurolinguistic Chunking — groups acoustic units into processable segments at a defined chunk-capacity window.

[0008] In a preferred embodiment, multimedia sources (motion pictures, television, dialogue recordings) are ingested during the SLPL phase, providing naturalistic prosody, rhythm, social turn-taking, and co-occurring audio-visual context. At this stage, content is processed for structural and prosodic properties only; no semantic content is extracted. audit Only licensed or lawfully usable material is ingested for training.

Section II — The Language Expression Layer (LEL) (FIG. 1)

[0009] The LEL is a trained model acquiring all linguistic capabilities as internalized learned parameters, without reliance upon external dictionaries, pronunciation databases, rule-based grammar engines, morphological analyzers, or language packs at inference. audit Structured linguistic resources may be used as training inputs, but are internalized as parameters rather than retained as runtime lookup tables. The training corpus comprises multilingual text; phonetic data and transcriptions; pronunciation patterns; grammar structures; semantic alignment datasets; speech corpora; grapheme-to-phoneme mappings; phonology datasets; and multimedia corpora.

[0010] The LEL acquires, as learned parameters, lexical, morphological, grammatical/syntactic, semantic, idiomatic, phonetic/phonological, pronunciation, and prosodic knowledge. audit The LEL is a multimodal (audio, text, video) training program whose duration is set by available compute and data and is established empirically. Corpus exposure is sequenced by the Transition Gate of Section III, which suppresses semantic alignment data during early training.

Section III — Developmental Learning Sequence and Transition Gate (FIG. 3)

[0011] Phase One (Structural-Acoustic): the LEL develops sound discrimination, syllabic segmentation, and word-as-acoustic-object recognition, with semantic alignment data suppressed as an explicit architectural constraint enforced by the Transition Gate.

[0012] The Transition Gate is a one-way measurable threshold mechanism. Phase Two commences only when all four thresholds are satisfied, each defined operationally with a calibratable target and a bounded no-convergence fallback audit:

ThresholdDefinitionMetric

[0013] Phase Two (Semantic-Communicative): upon satisfaction of all thresholds, semantic alignment data is activated and the system performs word-to-referent grounding using co-occurrence of acoustic units with visual and contextual events; communicative discovery (learning that word production causes structured responses); and emergence of grammar, syntax, and semantic structure from distributional patterns rather than imposed rules.

Section IV — Multilingual Transfer and the Acquisition Flywheel (FIG. 4)

[0014] Following first-language (L1) acquisition, the system employs a Delta Acquisition Model for subsequent languages, learning only the genuinely novel elements while reusing L1 phonemic discrimination, prosodic sensitivity, morphological awareness, communicative understanding, grammatical meta-knowledge, and semantic inventory. A multilingual semantic alignment layer bridges L2 lexical items to existing L1 concepts.

[0015] Genuinely novel L2 elements — phonemes absent from L1, grammatical structures without L1 analogue, culturally grounded idioms, and differing prosodic systems — receive targeted training. audit Acquisition cost generally decreases as shared structure accumulates (an acquisition flywheel); the trend is directional rather than strictly monotonic, and negative-transfer cases between particular language pairs are detected and resolved by targeted retraining.

Section V — The Speech Output Pipeline (FIG. 5)

[0016] The Speech Output Pipeline converts internalized representations into natural speech through seven sequential stages, each driven exclusively by learned parameters: (1) lexical selection from internalized lexical-semantic space without dictionary lookup; (2) context-sensitive grapheme-to-phoneme conversion, irregular forms handled by pattern matching; (3) coarticulation modeling; (4) prosody generation at syllable, word, phrase, sentence, and discourse levels; (5) register and affect calibration; (6) a self-monitoring feedback loop evaluating the generated output against internalized acoustic targets and a defined naturalness metric audit prior to emission; and (7) neural vocoder rendering, which renders only what upstream stages determine.

Section VI — Co-Grounded Integration with FQL and UFCS (FIG. 6)

[0017] The LEL is integrated with FQL and UFCS through a co-grounding relationship in which linguistic concepts and data entities share a common representational foundation. Natural-language input is analyzed by the LEL; the resulting semantic representations are mapped to FQL operations executed by the UFCS across federated data sources; results are rendered back to natural language, as text or speech, in the input language.

[0018] Because input in any supported language resolves to the same FQL representation, the query layer is language-agnostic. audit The correspondence between LEL semantics and the FQL schema is realized as a hybrid of an emergent structural correspondence, arising from the developmental grounding sequence, and, where necessary, an explicit semantic-alignment mapping layer between LEL concepts and FQL entities, relationships, and operations.

Section VII — Reduced-to-Practice Embodiment (Structural and Deterministic Semantic Phases) (FIG. 7, FIG. 8) rev 2026-09-29

[0019] A working embodiment of Phase One (structural-acoustic) of Section III has been reduced to practice as a deterministic, dependency-free software implementation (the “Language Lab”). The implementation ingests raw text and computes defined, measurable structural representations comprising: a grapheme inventory with frequencies; a rule-seeded grapheme-to-phoneme approximation providing an initial phoneme inventory; a syllabic segmentation; a lexical-stability measure; a word co-occurrence density; and prosodic proxies derived from punctuation and sentence structure. Each computed measurement is written as a provenance-tracked record (a Fact Unit) into a content-addressed, de-duplicated store, such that every structural observation carries a source, a confidence value, and a content-derived fingerprint.

[0020] The Transition Gate of Section III is embodied by evaluating four computed metrics — phonemic-inventory completeness, lexical stability, co-occurrence density, and pattern saturation — against calibratable thresholds, the gate latching one-way upon simultaneous satisfaction of all four. In the embodiment, structural learning is driven autonomously by a background agent that repeatedly ingests corpus material on a schedule, advancing the four metrics until the gate opens, at which point Phase One is complete. The embodiment demonstrates the developmental suppression of semantics during Phase One and the one-way gate mechanism.

[0021] In a further reduced-to-practice extension, Phase Two semantic grounding is embodied deterministically and without any large language model (FIG. 8). For words learned in Phase One, the embodiment attaches meaning from two auditable sources: (a) a bundled public-domain dictionary (for example, Webster’s Unabridged Dictionary of 1913), writing a provenance-tracked record of the form english word “w” · means · <definition> at a defined confidence with the dictionary as source; and (b) self-grounding, linking the word to verified Fact Units in which it already appears, of the form english word “w” · grounded_by_fact · <subject predicate object>. Each grounded meaning is a provenance-tracked Fact Unit carrying an auditable source; no large language model participates in the grounding path. An optional “suggestor” may propose a candidate meaning only where it agrees with the public-domain dictionary, which remains the authority.

[0022] The embodiment realizes the developmental phases as distinct agent roles upon a shared agent manager: a Phase-One structural agent and a Phase-Two deterministic semantic-grounding agent, both implemented and operative; and Phase-Three multilingual-delta and Phase-Four speech-output agents, which are specified and, pending their respective engines, decline to execute rather than produce unsupported output. audit This prototype evidences enablement of the sub-language priming of Section I, the structural-acoustic phase and transition gate of Section III, the deterministic semantic grounding of Phase Two, and the co-grounding of structural observations with the federated store. The trained neural LEL of Section II, the Delta Acquisition Model of Section IV, and the Speech Output Pipeline of Section V are described as preferred embodiments and are not asserted to be reduced to practice by this prototype; the deterministic dictionary-and-store semantic grounding of Phase Two is reduced to practice as described in FIG. 8, whereas the trained-neural form of Phase-Two grounding remains a preferred embodiment; the rule-seeded grapheme-to-phoneme mapping is an initial seeding inventory and not a learned grapheme-to-phoneme model; and language understanding and fact grounding throughout are achieved without any large language model. Where an LLM is employed at all — to phrase answers over already-verified facts, or to propose hypotheses and simulations — it operates in a non-asserting role and never establishes a fact the system has not independently verified against the deterministic store.

[0023] The embodiment further reduces to practice a family of reasoning agents on the shared agent manager, each observing the assertion boundary of the Prime Directive. A hypothesis agent and a simulation agent employ a large language model in a strictly non-asserting role — the former proposing candidate facts, the latter projecting the consequences of a scenario — whereupon each candidate is independently verified against the deterministic store (recorded as corroborated, contradicted, or open) and retained in isolation at a confidence below the assertion threshold, so that nothing proposed by the model is ever returned as a verified fact. A gated reasoning agent embodies the principle that deduction may assert while induction and abduction may only propose: it computes a deterministic transitive closure over verified premises — for a relation defined as transitive, from verified X R Y and Y R Z it asserts X R Z, each derived record carrying its two premises as provenance and a confidence that decays with each inferential hop so that chains terminate — requiring no large language model, because the conclusion is entailed by facts already verified; while inductive and abductive candidates are proposed and verified in the same non-asserting manner as the hypothesis agent. audit An answer gate distinguishes multi-valued relations (for example is_a, part_of, located_in), for which a subject may hold several objects concurrently without contradiction, from functional relations, for which competing objects are reported as a contradiction.

Abstract

A system and method for hierarchical developmental language acquisition. A Sub-Language Priming Layer establishes acoustic, phonemic, prosodic, communicative, and information-theoretic foundations prior to language corpus training. A Language Expression Layer (LEL) acquires all linguistic capabilities — phonetics, phonology, morphology, grammar, syntax, semantics, idioms, and pronunciation — as internalized learned parameters from a structured multilingual corpus including speech and multimedia, without external dictionaries, rule engines, or language packs. A Transition Gate of four measurable thresholds governs progression from structural-acoustic to semantic-communicative learning. A Delta Acquisition Model exploits first-language structural transfer to accelerate multilingual acquisition. A Speech Output Pipeline comprising grapheme-to-phoneme conversion, coarticulation modeling, prosody generation, register calibration, self-monitoring feedback, and neural vocoder rendering produces natural speech from internalized parameters. The LEL is co-grounded with a Federated Query Language and Universal Federated Connectivity System, enabling language-agnostic natural-language input to resolve to structured query execution, with results rendered as natural language or speech in the language of the original query.
